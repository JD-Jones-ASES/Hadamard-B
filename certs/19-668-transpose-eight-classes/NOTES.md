# cert 19 — the order-668 record is not equivalent to its transpose; eight matrices, still four transpose-extended classes

**Label: PROVEN.** Default run:
`python certs/19-668-transpose-eight-classes/run.py` from the repository root.
Standard library only, **≈ 1.3 s**, exit 0, **166 checks**. That run **audits a
banked exact computation**; `--full` recomputes the one new profile here. The
trust boundary is the one certs 06, 08, 13 and 15 draw, and is set out below.

This certificate adds no new construction. It adds **one exact 4-profile** —
that of `Hᵀ`, the transpose of the decoded order-668 record — and with it the
one comparison every earlier certificate at this order left open.

## The theorems

> **Theorem (the transpose).** Let `H` be the decoded `(s,i) = (1,1)` bordered
> Goethals–Seidel record at order 668 (certs 01/06). Then **`H` is not
> Hadamard-equivalent to `Hᵀ`**: the exact `|T4|` 4-profiles of the two
> matrices differ in **49** of the 80 bins `H` populates.

> **Theorem (eight).** Let `H'` be the Lemma-T `i = 2` rebuild (certs 02/06),
> `H★` the paired Hall switch of the public preprint (cert 08), and `H″` the
> orientation switch of `H` (cert 13). Then the **eight** matrices
> `H, H', H★, H″, Hᵀ, (H')ᵀ, (H★)ᵀ, (H″)ᵀ` are **pairwise Hadamard-
> inequivalent** — all 28 profile comparisons separate, the least separated
> pair by 24 bins. **Order 668 therefore carries at least eight Hadamard
> equivalence classes under plain Hadamard equivalence.**

> **Theorem (four, unchanged).** Under the **transpose-extended** relation
> (`A ≈ B` iff `A ~ B` or `A ~ Bᵀ`) those same eight matrices form **exactly
> four** classes — the four of certs 08, 13 and 15. Each pair `{X, Xᵀ}` is one
> class *by definition*; the four classes are pairwise separated by the
> two-refutation rule. **Nothing here is new under that relation.**

*Proof.* The multiset `{|T4|}` over all `C(668,4) = 8 222 179 035` row
4-subsets is a Hadamard-equivalence invariant (`note/NOTE-B.md` §3.1,
invariant **I5**), and the transpose of a Hadamard matrix is Hadamard, so
`profile(Xᵀ)` is that same invariant computed on that other matrix. An
invariant that differs is a separation; all 28 comparisons differ, in two
arithmetics that agree bin for bin. For the transpose-extended count:
`X ≈ Xᵀ` holds by definition, so there are **at most** four classes; and for
each of the six pairs of classes both refutations (`A` vs `B` and `A` vs `Bᵀ`)
are nonzero, so there are **at least** four. ∎

## Why `H ~ Hᵀ` was a real question

Hadamard equivalence is generated by row and column negations and
permutations: `B = D_r P_r A P_c D_c`. **The transpose is not in that group.**
So `profile(Hᵀ)` is not a restatement of `profile(H)` — it is the same
invariant computed on a *different matrix*, and whether the two matrices lie
in the same class is a question that no amount of row-side computation
answers. It is exactly the question cert 15 answered for `H″`, cert 08 for
`H'` and `H★`, and left open for `H` itself.

Nor is it idle. The bordered Goethals–Seidel family is **closed under
transposition** — the source laboratory's skeptic pass II derives `Hᵀ` as
another house-orientation member of the same family (reverse the type-1 seed,
negate one of the others, transpose the corner, transpose-and-swap the tables),
so `Hᵀ` is not a foreign object but a sibling record, and "same class or not"
is the same kind of question as `H` vs `H'`. That derivation is **intel from
the source laboratory** (`Hadamard-2060`, private:
`intel/fleet-2026-09-01/skeptic-pass/TRANSPOSE.md`, Theorem T, 2026-09-02);
it is **cited, not ported, and is not a claim of this repository** — nothing
in this certificate depends on it, and nothing in this repository proves it.
The same note left the count at 668 at "**at least 7**, exactly 8 iff
`H ≁ Hᵀ` — OPEN, `Hᵀ`'s profile was never computed". This certificate banks
that profile and closes it, by computation rather than by structure.

The same intel records that the analogous question is decided *both* ways at
small orders — `H ~ Hᵀ` at some orders 20–36, `H ≁ Hᵀ` at almost every
instance from 44 up. Again: cited as the reason the question is live, not as
evidence for the answer. The answer here is one exact enumeration.

## What "eight" does and does not mean

**It means:** eight matrices are exhibited at order 668, pairwise
Hadamard-inequivalent, each separation an exact `|T4|` 4-profile comparison
over all `C(668,4)` row 4-subsets in two arithmetics that agree bin for bin.
So order 668 carries **at least eight** equivalence classes under plain
Hadamard equivalence — a lower bound, from the artifacts in hand.

**It does not mean:**

* **that eight is the number of classes at 668.** It is a lower bound. The true
  number is unknown here and nothing in this repository bears on it.
* **that eight is a new count of constructions.** The eight matrices come from
  *four* objects and one involution. Four of them are transposes; a transpose
  is free once its original is in hand, which is exactly why the house does not
  headline the number.
* **anything new under the transpose-extended relation.** There the count is
  **four**, the count of certs 08, 13 and 15, and it is unchanged. Under that
  relation a matrix and its transpose are the same object by fiat, so the four
  transposes contribute nothing. The house counts four, because four is the
  count that survives *either* convention — the same posture cert 15 took at
  716, where it counted three and recorded six as a remark.
* **that transposes are generally inequivalent to their originals.** A
  Sylvester matrix *is* its transpose, so the profiles agree by force; and this
  run measures Paley I `H(20)` agreeing with its transpose bin for bin — a
  failure to separate, never a proof of equivalence. What is settled here is
  one matrix at one order.

## What is NOT claimed

* **The default run recomputes nothing.** `--full --impl blas` **has** been
  run here and binds the new bank to the matrix by computation; the `bits` leg
  has not been run in this repository.
* **Nothing at 716, 1676, 1772 or 2060.** `Hᵀ` was computed at 716 for cert 15,
  where the analogous plain-equivalence remark is six matrices from three
  constructions; 1676 and 1772 were still running when this certificate was
  written, and 2060's transposes had not started. All have since landed and
  carry their own certificates (21, 24, 25), and at each of those three
  orders one transpose was **never** profiled — `Hᵀ` at 1676 and 1772, `Gᵀ`
  at 2060 — so the eight-class plain-equivalence count below is 668's alone;
  elsewhere the analogous remark is five matrices, not eight. Nothing here
  depends on any of that.
* **No general theorem about transposes.** That `H ≁ Hᵀ` at 668 and at 716 is
  two orders. The structure theorem that makes `Hᵀ` a member of the same family
  is the source laboratory's, cited above, and is not proved, ported or relied
  on here.
* **No novelty or priority claim of any kind.** Order 668's "≥ 2 classes"
  statement is the anonymous preprint's (cert 08, `note/NOTE-B.md` §3.4);
  existence at this order is the announcing team's. This certificate counts
  classes among the artifacts banked here.
* **No claim that eight is the count** at 668, nor that four is the count under
  the transpose-extended relation. Both are lower bounds exhibited by the
  matrices in hand — with the one "exactly" in this certificate being local:
  exactly four transpose-extended classes **among these eight matrices**.
* **Matching invariants prove nothing.** `dim W` agrees on all four objects
  rebuilt here, `dim V` agrees too, and Paley `H(20)`'s profile agrees with its
  transpose's. Read every "agrees" in this repository as "did not separate",
  never as "the same".
